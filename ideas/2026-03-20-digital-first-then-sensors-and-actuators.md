# Digital work changes first, then the sensors and actuators, then atoms

Karpathy expects three phases. First, a large "overhang" of digital work is rewritten, because
copying bits is far cheaper than moving matter. Second, once agents have used up what is already
online, they need new data. The interface between digital and physical grows: sensors that feed
the intelligence and actuators that act for it. His examples are lab equipment for materials
science and paid human data collection. He also wants information markets, where an agent pays
for a photo from a given place. Third, robotics, which he thinks lags because of capital cost, as
in self-driving. He still thinks the physical market may be larger.

## Apply

- Rank your own project ideas by how much of the work is digital. Start there.
- Look for cheap sensors you can connect to an agent: cameras, logs, lab data, human labelers.
- Design agent tasks that name a price for a piece of data and let the agent find it.
