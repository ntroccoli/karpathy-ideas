# People judge AI differently because they use different AI

Karpathy sees a growing gap in how people understand AI. One group tried a free or old model, saw
silly errors and formed a view from that. Another pays for top agentic models and uses them in
code, maths and research, where models restructure a code base in an hour or find security holes.
The groups speak past each other, and both are partly right. Progress is fastest where rewards can
be checked, which suits reinforcement learning, and these areas also bring the most business
money. In October 2026 he refined it into a funnel, by rough numbers: about 75% of people have
barely used LLMs, about 20% use free chat now and then, about 20 million professionals use frontier
models for maths and code, and about 5,000 people inside labs see swarms of agents on huge
projects. Impact also grows with the size of the problem you give it.

## In his words

> "the people in these two groups are speaking past each other"
> — Andrej Karpathy, 2026-04-09

## Apply

- Before you judge a model for your work, test the current best paid model on your own task, with
  tool access.
- Try frontier models on your biggest, most verifiable problem, not on a one-paragraph question.
- When you argue about AI with someone, ask which tier they use.
- For soft tasks (writing, advice), expect slower gains and add your own review.
- Plan for review capacity: he says human review is starting to fall behind agent output.
