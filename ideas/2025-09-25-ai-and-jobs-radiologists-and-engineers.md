# Jobs change before they vanish, and supervision grows

Using radiologists as the example, Karpathy argues that AI and jobs is more complex than benchmarks
show. Benchmarks are narrow, jobs have many parts, and deployment meets friction. Demand may rise
as work gets cheaper (the Jevons effect). The most exposed work is repetitive, independent, closed,
short, forgiving and digital. In his Dwarkesh interview he called radiology a bad prediction: the
job is messy, with patients and many other duties. A better candidate is the call centre. Each call
is a similar short task with small context, and it is all digital. Even there he expects an
autonomy slider, not a clean swap: an AI might handle 80% of the volume and pass 20% to people, who
supervise teams of AIs. He expects software engineers to keep working, but to move toward
supervising automation.

## Apply

- Split a job into tasks. Score each by length, context needed and cost of an error.
- Automate first the tasks that repeat, need little context and have a check you can run.
- Design the human role as supervisor and exception handler, with a view of all AI actions.
- Move your own work toward writing specs, checks and reviews.
- Re-check results after a few months before you cut human staff.
