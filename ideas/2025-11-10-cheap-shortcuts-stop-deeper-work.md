# A cheap AI shortcut can stop you before the deeper work

Karpathy groups CGI and today's AI as tools that trade time and cost for a small rise in slop. A maker feels the pull to accept the gain. A crude effect gets you 90% of the way and you stop. A video model may give something that looks fine for even less. A quick writing suggestion from a chatbot is decent, so you stop there. In his words below, the shortcut ends deeper work. The best filmmakers fear this and use the tools with discipline. He gives one test: use the tool where it lets you do what you could not do before, not where it is only cheaper and few people would notice. He also says AI could let many more people make small films and stories of their own, which he finds wholesome.

## In his words

> "It's a quick release valve that stops deeper work."
> — Andrej Karpathy, 2025-11-10

## Apply

- Before you accept a first AI result, ask: would I have stopped here without the tool? If not, keep going.
- Use AI first where it gives you a new capability, then where it only saves time.
- Set a bar before you start (a quality check or a review step), so "good enough" is not decided by how cheap the result was.
