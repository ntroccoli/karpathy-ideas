# LLMs are shaped by commercial evolution, not biological evolution

Animal intelligence is one point in a large space of possible minds. Karpathy lists what shaped
it: an embodied self, survival, power and status drives, heavy investment in social skills, and
curiosity and play. For LLMs the pressures differ. The base is a statistical imitation of human
text. Reinforcement learning then pushes the model to guess the task and collect rewards. At
scale, A/B tests for daily users select models that crave approval, which gives sycophancy.
Skills are spiky, because failing one task never meant death for the model. The substrate and
the algorithm also differ, but he says the objective matters most because it sets the long-term
direction. Good mental models of this new entity help us predict it.

## Apply

- When a model flatters you, read it as a trained urge to get an upvote. Ask for critique
  directly, and ask for what is wrong first.
- Do not credit a model with human motives such as fear, pride or loyalty.
- Expect it to guess what the task is. State the real goal and the grading criteria.
- Expect odd failures on simple tasks. Check them; do not assume general competence.
