# AI can make governments more legible and accountable

Karpathy is optimistic about people using AI to see their governments more clearly. Governments
usually make society legible to themselves. AI lets society do the same in reverse. He argues
that accountability was not limited by access, since governments publish huge amounts of data. It
was limited by the intelligence needed to process it, as with a 4,000-page omnibus bill that is
open on paper but unreadable in practice. His examples: spending and budgets, diffs of
legislation, voting records against stated positions, lobbying graphs, procurement, campaign
finance. Local government may be the best target, since it gets less national coverage. He warns
that the same tools can be used the other way.

## In his words

> "it has been constrained by intelligence"
> — Andrej Karpathy, 2026-04-04

## Apply

- Pick one public data source (a city council's agendas, a budget file) and ask an agent to
  summarise changes since last year, with page citations.
- Have the agent diff two versions of a bill and list what changed in plain English.
- Check each claim against the source text before you publish it.
