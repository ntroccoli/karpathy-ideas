# LLMs are ghosts, not animals

After he heard Richard Sutton on a podcast, Karpathy gave his own view. Sutton wants agents that
learn from experience alone, like animals, and doubts that LLMs fit that ideal. Karpathy agrees
that today's systems are full of human input: human text, curated finetuning data and
human-tuned training environments. He calls the result "ghosts", imperfect replicas of humanity.
He does not think animals are a from-scratch example either. A baby zebra runs within minutes;
evolution gave its brain a strong starting point. Pretraining on internet text plays that role
for a network, as a crude substitute. He does not know if ghosts will drift toward animals or
away, like planes and birds. He still wants animal ideas for agents: curiosity, play, self-play
and culture.

## In his words

> "Pretraining is our crappy evolution."
> — Andrej Karpathy, 2025-10-01

## Apply

- Do not expect an LLM to have stable drives or a smooth skill level. Test what it does on
  your task.
- Explain model behaviour from its training (human text, task rewards, user votes), not from
  human motives. Threats and praise are not a technique.
- When you judge a research idea, ask what plays the starting prior and what plays later
  learning. Do not take animal examples as proof that a blank-slate learner works.
- Memory files such as CLAUDE.md are a text-based form of learning during use.
