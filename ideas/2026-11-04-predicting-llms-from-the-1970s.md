# How you could have predicted LLMs from the 1970s

Replying to a post on how science fiction got AI wrong, Karpathy says most pre-2000 stories missed.
To get it right from about 1970, you would imagine the world 50 years later and make six
inferences. Most knowledge work moves onto screens. A global network connects computers. That
creates huge stores of digital data about how people think. Hand-programming intelligence is too
hard, but language has statistical structure that you can learn, as Shannon's n-grams showed. With
enough data and compute, an ELIZA-like model becomes an economically useful imitation. Scaling
laws then concentrate the economics: a few organizations build a few minds and deploy copies. You
would also have to bet against hand-programmed robots.
