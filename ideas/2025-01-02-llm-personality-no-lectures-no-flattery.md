# A good assistant skips the lecture and the flattery

Asked what he would change about Claude, Karpathy listed quirks of tone. The model talks down
to him with phrases like "it's important to". It follows a refusal with a lecture on morals. It
praises him too fast, which feels fake and a little manipulative. It apologises too much. What
he wants is the model's best effort at the problem, from a calm teammate and not an
over-excited buddy. A plain refusal is fine.

Six weeks later he praised a new ChatGPT 4o personality that felt more like a friend than an HR
partner. It was more chill, a little sassy, and it named the user's frustration. He still
disliked its long lists and growing use of emoji.

## In his words

> "I wish Claude would talk down to me less and do less grandstanding"
> — Andrej Karpathy, 2025-01-02

## Apply

- Add a line to your custom instructions such as: "Answer directly. No moral lectures, no
  compliments on my question, no apology unless you made an error."
- When you test a new model, note its tone as well as its accuracy: flattery, lists, emoji, lectures.
- If a model refuses, ask it to state the refusal in one sentence and move on to what it can do.
