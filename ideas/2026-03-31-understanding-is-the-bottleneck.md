# You can outsource thinking, but not understanding

A reader wrote that you can outsource your thinking but not your understanding. Karpathy said he
thinks about this often, because he feels it become the bottleneck. What is in his head is private
to the LLM. It does not know what he knows, and he does not know what he does not know, so the
"mind meld" works only moderately well. He returned to the line at Sequoia Ascent 2026. Even if
agents do more of the work, a human must know what is worth building, which question matters,
which result looks wrong and which trade-off is fine. This is why he likes LLM knowledge bases:
they turn information into understanding, not only answers. A reply on his wiki workflow makes
the same point: LLM tools let you skip writing, but not reading and thinking.

## In his words

> "The mind meld is only medium effective."
> — Andrej Karpathy, 2026-03-31

## Apply

- After an agent finishes a task, ask it to explain the design and the risky parts. Check that you
  can state them in your own words.
- After the LLM summarises a document, read the summary and the source passages that matter.
- Tell the model what you already know before you ask. State your level and your gaps.
- Ask the model to quiz you, or to list what it assumed you knew.
- Distil your own field into a small, readable artifact (one file, one page) that an agent can
  teach from, as he did with microGPT.
