# The bitter lesson as "turn the crank"

He explains the bitter lesson as a preference for algorithms where you only turn a crank. When you get more compute, which is a faster crank, you should not have to change anything: you run it faster and the result improves. You can break this rule on purpose, and locally that is often right. The risk is that you may not be around when the new, faster crank arrives.

## In his words

> "you just crank faster to make better."
> — Andrej Karpathy, 2025-06-26

## Apply

- When you add a hand-made trick to a system, ask whether it would still help, or would block gains, with ten times more compute.
- Keep such tricks easy to remove.
